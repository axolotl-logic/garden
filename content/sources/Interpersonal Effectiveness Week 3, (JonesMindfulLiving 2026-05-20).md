> Replay 5/20/26: Interpersonal Effectiveness Week 3, https://video.jonesmindfulliving.com/videos/interpersonal-effectiveness-week-3

The three goals of interpersonal effectiveness and their corresponding skills are figured below.

| Goal                       | Outcome                                             | Skill    |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Objective Effectiveness    | Get what you want. Have a "no" heard.               | DEAR MAN |
| Relationship Effectiveness | Healthy new and existing relationships.             | GIVE     |
| Self-Respect Effectiveness | Feeling good about one's self, especially behaviors | FAST     |

You can make a sacrifice of short-term goals to support long-term goals and *vice versa*. However, one must keep them in balance. Repeatedly short changing one for the other causes an eventual implosion or explosion of the relationship. On top of this, extreme and unskilled behaviors get what you want in the short term at a high cost of the long-term. It can be harmful to chronically put relationship needs over one's own needs or the other way around. In essence, one must be strategic around sacrifices.

This week focuses on the GIVE skill.

Be **G**entle
**A**ct Interested
**V**alidate
Use an **E**asy Manner

The two questions to task are:
1. How do you want them to feel about you after?
2. How do I maintain/obtain this relationship?

Validation/invalidation speaks to the emotional mind. One consequence of this is that invalidation can cause an escalation of tension. A person may seek validation forcefully having not received it.

Be aware of resentment building and act on it as a clue that validation is needed.

Avoid rapid fire texting, despite feeling like there's an emergency. This is disrespectful to the other person's needs and circumstance. It also does not cause them to respond sooner and does not undo what you've said. Putting away the phone is a valid skill. 

"I feel" over "You made me feel".

Leave gracefully.

Do not minimize or magnify their feelings, blame them, or proceed with fixing/problem solving without first acknowledging their experience. Ask if what is unsaid is true instead of assuming. This includes what the other person may want or feel. 

Act interested, listening to what they have to say. Look at the person, make eye contact. Don't talk over or interrupt. Act sensitive.

Validation is acknowledgement. 

Validate, *iff* valid/true. Some things a person might validate include feelings, emotions, beliefs, opinions, suffering, difficulties, and facts.

Give body language that you're hearing the other person.
