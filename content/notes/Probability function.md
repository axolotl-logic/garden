A probability function answers the question of how probable an event is. You can think of it as one way of measuring an event. This measurement is a number from 0 to 1,  where 0 the event wont happen and 1 it will.

Get out the red thread, because you can also think of a probability function as a mapping between two things. On the left you have every possible combination of things that could occur. There's stub your toe, eating cereal, doing both, doing neither[^1].  On the right you have the number line. Tie a string from each of the four things that could happen (each called and event) to a number on the number line between 0 and 1 (including 0 and 1). There's your probability function! Say you want to know the probability of only stubbing your toe and not getting to your cereal, you'd look at the "stubbing your toe" event on the left and follow the string to the number on the right. Remember events are sets of things that can happen.

Probability functions are a basic building block of probability theory. In fact, they are defined in the [[3 axioms of probability theory]] which is the foundation from which the rest is built.

TLDR; Maps events in a [[Sigma-algebra]] to the real numbers between $0..1$.
